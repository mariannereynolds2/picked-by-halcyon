# 1. The Feeder Legacy — Louisa Masters
**Proposed genre:** Fantasy · Romance
**Editorial label:** Queer Paranormal Fantasy
**Suggested one-line introduction:** A career reset becomes a crash course in vampire society when Adam inherits a family profession nobody told him existed.
**Feature headline:** A family business with a cost no employment contract can explain

There is something quietly disarming about a character who has already failed at several perfectly ordinary jobs being offered an extraordinary one. Adam isn't hunting vampires or looking to become part of a supernatural prophecy. He needs work. His estranged family has a solution: an inherited profession as a vampire feeder, with generous pay, accommodation and the small matter of offering his blood twice a day.

That arrangement gives *The Feeder Legacy* its most interesting question. A job can look like a gift when a person has run out of choices, but how much does Adam really know about what he is agreeing to? His new employer, Tristan, assumes that Adam understands the complicated rules of a world his parents deliberately concealed from him. Adam knows better than to admit he is completely unprepared. The tension rests not only in vampire politics, but in watching someone navigate a hierarchy while concealing the very ignorance that makes him vulnerable.

Louisa Masters brings the irreverence familiar from her paranormal romances to a more structured new vampire society. Rather than suggesting this first novel resolves everything, its early reception points toward extensive worldbuilding and an unfolding central relationship. That is part of the recommendation: readers who like entering an elaborate supernatural setting at its beginning will find more reason to start here than readers wanting a brisk, fully self-contained romantic conclusion.

**For readers who enjoy:** Character-led vampire worlds, queer paranormal fantasy, complicated supernatural hierarchies, wry voices, and series that reward following a developing mythology.

**Before you begin:** First installment of *The Vampire Dominion*. An independent release-day review reports substantial setup and a cliffhanger; avoid promising a complete, single-volume resolution.

# 2. Salome — Hanna Delaney
**Proposed genre:** Horror & Science Fiction · Fantasy
**Editorial label:** Victorian Gothic Horror
**Suggested one-line introduction:** In 1885, a condemned novice is offered a second life as a vampire hunter—but her greatest battle may be over the meaning of her own faith.
**Feature headline:** When salvation and temptation both ask for your obedience

The first unsettling question in *Salome* is not whether vampires exist. It is what happens to a young nun after she confesses to killing another sister. In Turin in 1885, the expected answer is a death sentence. Instead, Salome is taken to Liverpool and placed with a clandestine religious order whose work crosses into demonology and vampire hunting. Her punishment has become a vocation, though whether the people guiding her are offering redemption or another kind of control remains a more difficult question.

This is where Hanna Delaney's particular historical setting matters. Her wider Muldoon mysteries inhabit late-Victorian Liverpool, a port city suited to stories of hidden networks and uneasy boundaries. But *Salome* stands apart: a religious Gothic narrative seen through the eyes of the novice herself, set years before Daniel Muldoon's occult investigations. Readers do not need the earlier mysteries to follow her.

The recurring nightmare Salome cannot escape gives the book its personal centre. She has an unusual gift; the Church believes it could serve the struggle against evil; and the forces pursuing her are prepared to offer consolation of their own. Delaney's essays about the novel trace an interest in classic vampire fiction and the connection between fear, faith and moral choice. Those ideas make this more than a story about a nun learning to fight monsters. It asks who gets to decide what a gifted but wounded young person owes to the world.

**For readers who enjoy:** Victorian Gothic atmosphere, religious horror, conflicted heroines, occult mysteries, vampire mythology and ethical questions without easy answers.

**Before you begin:** An independent standalone prequel within the wider Muldoon setting. Faith, murder, temptation and demonic threat are central themes.

# 3. Siren Song — Taylor E. Weston
**Proposed genre:** Fantasy · Romance
**Editorial label:** Adult Queer Sports Fantasy Romance
**Suggested one-line introduction:** Aleks has fought to return to artistic swimming; qualifying for the Clash of Creatures means trusting the coach whose disappearance still hurts.
**Feature headline:** The hardest part of returning to the water may be accepting help

The competition is the visible goal in *Siren Song*, but the more difficult contest begins long before Aleks Parthenope reaches the pool. An outcast siren and trans man, he has worked toward competing again in artistic swimming after years of rejection. Qualifying for the Clash of Creatures would mean reclaiming more than a place in a sporting event. It would mean performing on his own terms in a world that has tried to make him feel he does not belong.

The obstacle is painfully practical: Aleks needs a coach, and no one wants to train him. Javier Zamora, whose own family history connects him to sirens, reappears after leaving Aleks's life years earlier. Javier has carried guilt linked to the death of another young siren, but what he owes Aleks is not his to decide. The premise creates a sharp emotional question beneath the training: can two people build trust around a shared ambition when their history makes even accepting help difficult?

Taylor E. Weston's earlier work is rooted in character-driven sports romance, and this novella moves that interest into a magical arena without abandoning the pressures of athletic identity or personal dignity. The pairing of a monster-sport world with a recognizably human struggle for a second chance is what gives this story its editorial appeal.

**For readers who enjoy:** Queer and trans-led fantasy romance, competitive sports, second chances, coaches and athletes with history, and explicitly adult, emotionally resolved love stories.

**Before you begin:** The author describes this as a standalone romance within the shared *Clash of Creatures* setting. Adult explicit sexual content and a daddy/boy dynamic are part of the work; check the author's full content notes.

# 4. Fall Into Me — Becca Neil
**Proposed genre:** Romance
**Editorial label:** Contemporary MM · Grief and Found Family
**Suggested one-line introduction:** A winter breakdown leaves Asher caring for his infant niece and accepting help from a stranger who knows what it means to put his own life on hold.
**Feature headline:** Two caregivers discover that being needed is not the same as being cared for

In *Fall Into Me*, there is no shortage of people who need someone. Asher Finnley loses his twin sister after already surviving the deaths of his parents and suddenly becomes responsible for her baby daughter, Bri. On the journey back toward Seattle, grief, exhaustion and a December snowstorm leave him stranded in California with a child he is only beginning to learn how to care for.

Caspian Jacobs understands the habit of putting his own plans aside. He returned to Redding when his mother's health deteriorated and became her full-time caregiver. When he meets Asher, he offers help without knowing how much that gesture will change either of their lives. Yet the moment of rescue is not the entire relationship. Once the roads reopen, the two men must find out whether a friendship formed in crisis can survive distance, complicated obligations and the uneven pace of grief.

Becca Neil's earlier romances repeatedly return to trust, trauma and the work of healing through another person's consistent presence. Here that concern is particularly grounded: a four-month-old child still needs care, a disabled parent still needs care, and neither man can simply abandon what matters to him for a conventional romantic ending. That makes the novel well suited to readers who prefer an unhurried love story with real responsibilities around it.

**For readers who enjoy:** Slow-burn contemporary MM romance, found family, caregiving, fragile new friendships, long-distance love and character-centred emotional recovery.

**Before you begin:** A standalone with substantial themes of bereavement, parental death, care of an orphaned infant and anxiety/depression. Reader and release listings give October 15 or 16; confirm final publisher metadata before feature date is displayed.
