Ready or Not

by Chautona Havig

At twenty-two, Aggie becomes guardian to eight children. Love now asks for skills she has never had to learn.

On our shelves: Faith & Spirituality · Romance

WHY WE PICKED IT

Caring before you feel ready

A promise to care for someone can be sincere long before we know how to keep it. That is the question behind our choice of Ready or Not. Fresh out of college, Aggie Milliken loses her sister and brother-in-law and becomes guardian to their eight children. The future she expected gives way to a household that needs her now.

Havig gives that responsibility an everyday scale: learning to manage children and a home, navigating renovations, and dealing with a grandmother who challenges Aggie's place in the family. Those practical pressures make a useful starting point for discussion. What does a grieving family need from the adult who has stepped in? How can relatives offer help without making her feel that every mistake proves she should never have accepted the role?

We picked this for readers who want to consider faith through the work of looking after people. Aggie's confidence that God has called her to this family does not supply experience she lacks. That gap gives a reading group room to talk about willingness, competence and accepting support. It also invites a more personal question: when have we needed encouragement while still learning to do something important?

This is explicitly Christian fiction, with prayer and hymns woven into Aggie's response to difficulty. Its family comedy sits alongside bereavement and the disruption children experience after losing their parents. Readers seeking a secular domestic novel should know that faith is central. Ready or Not begins the four-book Aggie's Inheritance series, making it an accessible first meeting with this family rather than a promise to resolve their entire story in one volume.

THIS ONE IS FOR

Readers of Christian family fiction, church reading groups and book clubs interested in guardianship, grief and the difference between offering help and taking control. Begin with this first volume of Aggie's Inheritance.

MEET THE FEATURED AUTHOR

Chautona Havig

Chautona Havig writes Christian fiction from California's Mojave Desert. Her catalogue includes the interconnected Rockland stories, family fiction, mysteries and historical romance. On her website she describes encouragement in faith as a central purpose of her writing. Aggie's Inheritance is one of her suggested starting points for readers new to her work.
