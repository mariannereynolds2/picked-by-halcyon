Between the Avalanche and the Ashes

by Dorota Chioma

A visual artist navigates the extremes of bipolar disorder, discovering that creativity can be both a refuge and a risk.

The story

About the book

Between the Avalanche and the Ashes is an intimate memoir of living with bipolar disorder, navigating emotional extremes and rediscovering oneself through creativity.

Through personal reflections, diary entries and artistic milestones, Dorota Chioma takes readers through the exhilarating intensity of mania, the devastating weight of depression and the uncertain territory between them.

Art becomes a central thread throughout her journey—sometimes a sanctuary, sometimes an expression of overwhelming energy, and sometimes the only language available when words fail.

Rather than presenting recovery as a straightforward journey, the book explores its complexity, contradictions and fragile moments of hope. It raises difficult questions about identity, creativity, illness and the cost of pursuing passion while protecting one’s wellbeing.

Ultimately, it is a testament to the courage of continuing, creating and finding meaning in the aftermath.

Why readers may enjoy it

Readers gain an honest understanding of bipolar disorder from within, beyond clinical definitions and stereotypes. The combination of personal narrative and artistic expression offers companionship, recognition and hope to those navigating their own emotional struggles. It reminds readers that survival does not require a perfect recovery.

Who it suits

For adult readers interested in mental health, personal memoirs, creativity and resilience. Particularly relevant to people living with bipolar disorder, their loved ones and mental health professionals. Content notes: honest discussions of mania, psychosis, severe depression and suicidal experiences.

About the author

Dorota Chioma

Dorota Chioma is a Polish-born British-based visual artist, author and mental health advocate living with bipolar disorder. Her work explores the relationship between creativity, identity, emotional extremes and resilience. Through painting, memoir and reflective essays, she transforms lived experience into accessible conversations about mental health. Her books combine artistic expression with honest personal narratives, challenging stigma without romanticising illness or recovery. Alongside her creative practice, she works full-time. She believes that art is not merely a form of expression, but a means of survival, connection and rediscovery.
