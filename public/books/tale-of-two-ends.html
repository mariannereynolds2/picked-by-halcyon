Tale of Two Ends

by Edward M. Krauss

A marriage ends. Parenthood, work and the need to trust someone again carry on.

Amazon paperback ↗Amazon Kindle ↗ThriftBooks ↗

On our shelves: Literary Fiction · Romance

WHY WE PICKED IT

The life that continues after the marriage.

The part of this premise that earns our attention is everything a divorce cannot put on hold. A working mother discovers her husband's infidelity, but she still has a daughter to raise, a job to do and a mother whose health becomes another worry. Edward M. Krauss places the possibility of starting over inside a life already full of obligations.

That is a useful starting point for a relationship novel. Betrayal might happen between two people, but its consequences reach the whole household. A teenager can be hurting while her mother is hurting too. A family emergency can arrive before anyone has had time to recover. Even an encounter with the husband's lover belongs to the awkward, practical business of continuing to live in the same world.

We picked Tale of Two Ends for readers interested in that unsettled middle: after a relationship has broken, before a new life feels dependable. The author's description includes the uncomfortable return to dating, which raises a question more interesting than whether another partner will appear. How do you judge your own instincts after someone you trusted has made you doubt them?

Bring this one to a reading group that enjoys talking about second chances, parenting and the difference between forgiveness and reconciliation. Its premise gives those conversations somewhere concrete to begin. The reason to look closer is the woman rebuilding a life while other people still need her, including on the days when she needs help herself.

THIS ONE IS FOR

Readers of domestic fiction and relationship stories, especially reading groups interested in divorce, motherhood, trust and starting again.

MEET THE FEATURED AUTHOR

Edward M. Krauss

Edward M. Krauss is an Ohio novelist whose books include Tale of Two Ends, Solomon the Accountant, A Story of Bad and GeeCee. His author biography also describes his work as a mediator in the legal system. He welcomes invitations to speak with reading groups.
