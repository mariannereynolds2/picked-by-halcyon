Broken Souls and Bones

by LJ Andrews

A woman whose magic makes her valuable to the crown must decide what loyalty costs when power claims her life.

Why we picked it

Being valued is not the same as being free

A kingdom can praise a person's gift while taking away her right to decide how it will be used. That contradiction is our reason for choosing Broken Souls and Bones. Lyra Bien's power makes her important to the crown. Importance brings danger, and a title can become another name for captivity.

Lyra has tried to remain unnoticed. When Roark Ashwood, the prince's silent guard, enters her village, protecting her best friend forces her to reveal abilities she has kept hidden. The crown claims her as its next melder, a position described in the publisher's premise as both revered and feared, with a deadly cost. Her immediate problem is therefore larger than learning to use magic. She needs a way to keep herself and the people she cares about from becoming resources for someone else's rule.

For readers who enjoy fantasy romance, the relationship with Roark supplies a difficult question of its own. He first enters Lyra's life as her captor. As the story moves toward alliance and attraction, what would have to change before trust could be justified? That is a useful discussion precisely because attraction alone cannot answer it. Readers can examine actions, loyalties and choices, and disagree about the point at which they believe someone has earned another person's confidence.

The political premise also makes room for conversation beyond the romance. A ruler's demand can arrive dressed as honour. A dangerous duty can be described as a privilege. We are interested in the way that language changes what people believe they are allowed to refuse. A group can bring that question back to the world Andrews builds: who benefits from calling a sacrifice noble, and who has to live with the cost?

This is a substantial adult romantasy at 480 pages, with a darker world, violence and a passionate romantic element. It is the first half of a completed duology; Heir of Twisted Lies is the continuation. That makes it easier to plan a two-book read, but it should not be sold to a club as a standalone with every thread resolved. The author's reading-order page helps readers place it within her wider catalog without committing to the longer Broken Kingdoms sequence first.

FairyLoot included it in its romantasy subscription program, evidence of a specialist reader audience rather than a discussion-club award. Our recommendation rests on the questions the story sets up: whether usefulness can be mistaken for worth, whether loyalty survives coercion, and what two people must risk to become allies. Choose it for readers who want their romance inside a consequential fantasy conflict, and are happy to give that world two books of their time.

Questions for your reading group

Halcyon discussion starters. Discuss the text without feeling obliged to share personal experiences.

  1. When does recognition of a gift become a justification for exploiting its owner?
  2. What would Roark need to do for Lyra's trust to feel freely given?
  3. How does the story distinguish loyalty to a person from obedience to a ruler?

Before you buy

For adult romantasy readers who enjoy dark magic, dangerous loyalties and an intense romantic arc. Expect violence and sexual content. This is a 480-page opening volume, followed by Heir of Twisted Lies; it is not a standalone or a young-adult selection.

More from the author

Continue this story with Heir of Twisted Lies. For a separate entry into Andrews's larger fictional worlds, consult the official reading order for Curse of Shadows and Thorns and The Ever King.

About the author

LJ Andrews

LJ Andrews is a bestselling fantasy-romance author based in Utah. Her work draws on Nordic and Viking-inspired settings, and her catalog includes The Broken Kingdoms, The Ever Seas and the Broken Souls and Bones duology. Her official site hosts reading-order information, reader events and read-along material. Her published headshot is used here without alteration.
