Falling Like Snow

by Misty Wilson

A winter festival brings Sloane closer to a new crush—and makes her look twice at the best friend who has always been there.

View on Amazon (opens in a new tab)Read why we picked it

Why we picked it

When the right person might already know your heart

At Bramble Falls, a festival is more than an event on the calendar. It is a reason to see everyone, volunteer, make plans and face the complications of a small town where everyone seems to know everyone else's business. Sloane Sullivan expects the upcoming winter festival to provide the perfect holiday atmosphere. Instead, her own feelings become the hardest thing to organize.

Micah, a charming hockey player, offers the thrill of a new attraction. Asher, her closest friend, offers the comfort of being understood without explanation. The interesting question is not simply which boy Sloane might choose. It is whether another possibility has made her reconsider a friendship she has never needed to define as romance.

Wilson makes the tangle of feelings part of a larger world of seasonal obligations, family and belonging. It is easy to cheer for a heroine discovering a new crush; it is harder to decide what she owes a best friend when telling him the truth could change everything between them. For a reading group, this is a chance to talk about whether romance grows naturally out of friendship or transforms it into something riskier.

We picked Falling Like Snow for readers seeking young-adult romance with real emotional choices and a warm seasonal setting. It is part of the Bramble Falls series, following Falling Like Leaves; returning readers will recognize the town and its rhythms. The book was published October 6, 2026. Its appeal is first love and changing friendship, rather than the explicit content sometimes associated with adult hockey romance.

Who it’s for

Teen and adult readers who enjoy festive small-town settings, friends-to-lovers tension, first relationships and accessible book-club discussion. This is a Bramble Falls follow-up.

Book club conversation

If someone has been your safest friend for years, is the possibility of romance worth risking that friendship?

About the author

Misty Wilson

Misty Wilson is the New York Times bestselling author of the Bramble Falls series and Play Like a Girl, her award-winning middle-grade graphic memoir. A teacher and registered nurse, she also enjoys reading and romantic comedies. Wilson lives in Northeast Ohio with her family. Falling Like Snow reflects her interest in heartfelt young-adult stories where friendship, seasonal atmosphere and hard decisions meet.
