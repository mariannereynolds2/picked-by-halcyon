Stellar Smoke

Qamar returns to the island of her childhood seeking her mother—and encounters a machine that carries her mother's memories and unfinished history.

WHY WE PICKED IT

Can a machine tell you who your mother really was?

Qamar makes music in a precarious near-future city, trying to pay her rent while her personal life comes apart. A painful breakup pushes her back toward the place she has avoided for years: the island ashram where she grew up. She hopes to confront her estrangement from her mother, Veda. Instead, she arrives to find the grounds neglected and both her mother and younger sister missing.

Behind a secured door waits VEDA—Virtual Experiences, Dreams and Afterlife—a machine consciousness built as a reflection of her mother's life. It offers a history Qamar has never fully known, stretching across generations and the collapse of the spiritual community that once shaped her family. Yet the presence of a digital voice raises a question more troubling than whether a machine can speak: who has chosen what it remembers?

Stellar Smoke makes artificial intelligence personal. Many stories ask whether a machine is conscious; this one also asks how a daughter can recognize her mother in the memories a machine presents. The technology offers the possibility of intimacy while complicating what counts as testimony. There is room for a reading group to debate whether an artificial record can ever replace a difficult conversation with a living person.

We chose this book for literary science-fiction readers who want a speculative premise anchored in emotion, cultural history and inheritance. Tanaïs's wider creative practice spans literature, perfume and memory, bringing an unusual sensibility to the novel's world. The Dutton edition is scheduled for October 20, 2026; it is presented here as a preorder pick for mature readers, not as a full-book review.

THIS ONE IS FOR

Literary and adult science-fiction clubs interested in AI identity, mother–daughter estrangement, cultural memory, spiritual communities and the ethics of reconstructing someone else's life.

BOOK CLUB CONVERSATION

If a computer could tell your parent's life story in their own voice, would you believe it—and would understanding mean forgiving?

MEET THE FEATURED AUTHOR

Tanaïs

Tanaïs is a novelist, essayist, perfumer and multidisciplinary artist. Their novel Bright Lines received several first-novel prize nominations, and their nonfiction book In Sensorium: Notes for My People won the Kirkus Prize for Nonfiction. Their work often bridges embodiment, scent, South Asian history and imagination. They founded Studio Tanaïs and live in New York City. Stellar Smoke is their forthcoming speculative novel from Dutton.
