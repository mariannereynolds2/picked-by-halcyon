I Am Yours: A Shared Memoir

by Reema Zaman

A memoir about reclaiming a voice, and learning to distinguish belonging from being possessed.

Why we picked it

A voice that does not need permission

In the published extract from I Am Yours, a driving lesson becomes a struggle over who is allowed to direct a woman's life. Zaman sits at the wheel while her father and husband give conflicting instructions. Her request is simple: let her drive. The scene makes the book's concern with voice tangible. Speaking clearly is not always enough when the people listening believe they already know better.

That is why this memoir belongs on our reading desk. It gives a discussion group a precise place to begin: the difference between help and control. Readers do not need identical experiences to recognise that difference, or to disagree about how easily it can be disguised. The scene also shows the value of Zaman's prose, which turns an ordinary act into a moment of emotional and physical pressure without requiring the reader to begin with an abstract argument.

I Am Yours follows Zaman from childhood toward adulthood, through the expectations of family, relationships and the work of understanding her own experience. It addresses sexual violence, an eating disorder and emotional abuse. Those are substantial subjects, and readers deserve to know about them before purchase. The book's invitation is to attend to a particular woman's account, rather than to treat her life as a lesson that must stand for every woman or every culture.

The subtitle, A Shared Memoir, also matters to our selection. The book addresses an intimate 'you', bringing an inner conversation into the telling. That form gives readers something literary to discuss alongside the events: who seems to be listening, how the address changes the distance between writer and reader, and what it means to keep speaking when other people deny the authority of your account. A group can talk about the construction of the voice as carefully as it talks about the history behind it.

There is documented book-club interest here. Gemma Hartley announced an April 2024 discussion of I Am Yours with Zaman, and Feminist Book Club devoted an episode to the memoir. Those links offer further conversation, not an invented claim of a celebrity-club endorsement. They also make this a more concrete discussion-led choice than selecting a memoir simply because its author has a visible social following.

We recommend it to readers who are willing to spend time with a lyrical, demanding account of finding authority over one's own story. The question we would bring to the table is not why a survivor failed to speak sooner. It is what families, partners and institutions do with the words they are given. There is room here to talk about compassion, responsibility and the kind of belonging that leaves a person fully present.

Questions for your reading group

Halcyon discussion starters. Discuss the text without feeling obliged to share personal experiences.

  1. In the driving extract, what distinguishes an offer of help from an attempt to control?
  2. How does addressing a 'you' affect your position as a reader?
  3. What responsibilities do listeners have when someone describes an experience they would prefer not to believe?

Before you buy

For readers of literary memoir, intimate narrative voice and conversations about agency and belonging. Includes rape and sexual harassment, emotional abuse, anorexia and a car crash. Groups should let readers opt out of personal disclosure; discussion can remain with the text. This is a memoir, not a therapeutic guide.

About the author

Reema Zaman

Reema Zaman is an author, screenwriter and book coach, born in Bangladesh and raised in Hawaii and Thailand. Her memoir I Am Yours was published by Amberjack in 2019. Her essays have appeared in publications including Vogue, The Guardian and The Rumpus. She is based in Los Angeles and runs The Memoir Incubator.
